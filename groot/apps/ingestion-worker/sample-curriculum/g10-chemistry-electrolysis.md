# Grade 10 Chemistry — Chemical Reactions

## Electrolysis

Electrolysis is the process of using an electric current to drive a
non-spontaneous chemical reaction. It is the opposite of the reaction that
occurs in a galvanic (voltaic) cell.

### Key terms

- **Electrolyte**: a substance (often an ionic compound dissolved in water or
  molten) that conducts electricity because of its ions.
- **Anode**: the positive electrode; oxidation occurs here.
- **Cathode**: the negative electrode; reduction occurs here.
- **Electrolytic cell**: the apparatus in which electrolysis takes place.

### Electrolysis of water

When water is acidified and electrolyzed:

- At the cathode (reduction): 2 H₂O + 2 e⁻ → H₂ + 2 OH⁻
- At the anode (oxidation): 2 H₂O → O₂ + 4 H⁺ + 4 e⁻

The overall reaction is:

  2 H₂O → 2 H₂ + O₂

The volume of hydrogen produced at the cathode is twice the volume of oxygen
produced at the anode.

### Electrolysis of molten sodium chloride

In the Downs process, molten NaCl is electrolyzed to produce sodium metal
and chlorine gas:

- Cathode: Na⁺ + e⁻ → Na (liquid)
- Anode: 2 Cl⁻ → Cl₂ (gas) + 2 e⁻

This is the main industrial method for producing sodium.

### Industrial applications

- **Extraction of reactive metals** such as sodium, potassium, and aluminum.
- **Purification of copper** (electrorefining).
- **Electroplating** — coating one metal with another.
- **Production of chlorine and sodium hydroxide** (chlor-alkali process).

### Why it matters

Electrolysis is how society obtains many of the reactive metals and gases
that modern industry depends on. Without it, the production of aluminum,
for example, would be impractical at scale.
