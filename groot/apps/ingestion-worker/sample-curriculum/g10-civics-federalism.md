# Grade 10 Civics — Forms of Government

## Federalism

Federalism is a system of government in which power is divided between a
central (federal) government and regional governments (states, regions, or
provinces). Each level of government has its own areas of authority, and
neither level can simply abolish the other.

### Key features

- **Division of powers**: Some powers belong to the central government
  (defense, foreign policy, currency). Others belong to the regions
  (education, local policing, cultural affairs).
- **Constitutional protection**: The division of powers is written into the
  constitution and cannot be changed by ordinary law.
- **Independent courts**: A constitutional court typically resolves disputes
  between the central and regional governments.
- **Shared powers**: In some areas, both levels may act, with the central
  law prevailing in case of conflict.

### Federalism in Ethiopia

Ethiopia adopted a federal system in 1995 under the current Constitution.
The Federal Democratic Republic of Ethiopia is composed of:

- The Federal Government, based in Addis Ababa.
- Nine Regional States (Tigray, Afar, Amhara, Oromia, Somali, Benishangul-Gumuz,
  Southern Nations, Gambella, Harari).
- Two City Administrations (Addis Ababa and Dire Dawa).

Each region has its own regional council, executive, and judiciary. The
House of Federation represents the nations, nationalities, and peoples of
Ethiopia and has the power to interpret the Constitution.

### Federalism vs. unitary systems

In a **unitary** system, all power rests with the central government, which
may delegate authority to local bodies but can revoke it. In a **confederal**
system, power rests with the regional governments and the central body is
weak. Federalism sits between these two extremes.

### Why countries choose federalism

Federal systems are often chosen in large, diverse countries where different
regions have distinct languages, cultures, or economies. The goal is to balance
unity at the national level with autonomy at the regional level.
